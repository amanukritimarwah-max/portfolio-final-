C

Affinity mapping

120+ observations from the interviews, grouped on the board into five themes. Each theme ends in a design implication.

Theme 1Meaning seeking
Users wanted to understand rituals, symbols, architecture rather than simply visit temples.
Users ask “why” behind rituals and practices.
Interest in stories of temples, deities and architecture.
Desire for deeper cultural and spiritual context.
“I want to know the meaning behind what I do.”
“The stories behind temples fascinate me more.”
“Understanding makes the visit more meaningful.”
ImplicationDesign for curiosity and meaning. Provide stories, context, symbol explanations and architectural interpretation.
Theme 2Reflection
Many participants reported forgetting spiritual lessons after returning home.
Spiritual insights fade in daily routines.
Lack of prompts or reminders to reflect.
No habit formation or continuity tools.
“I feel so inspired at the temple, but forget it later.”
“There’s no reminder of what I learned.”
“I wish I could reflect more after returning home.”
ImplicationEnable reflection and continuity. Provide reminders, journaling prompts and spiritual takeaways.
Theme 3Fragmentation
Users relied upon multiple independent applications.
Different apps for booking, information, stories, etc.
Switching apps breaks the experience.
Information is scattered and not connected.
“I use one app to book, another to read, another for directions.”
“It’s confusing and time consuming.”
“I wish everything was in one place.”
ImplicationCreate a unified ecosystem. Integrate services and information for a seamless experience.
Theme 4Family traditions
Pilgrimage was often experienced collectively. Participants valued shared memories more than digital services.
Travel and rituals happen with family and friends.
Shared experiences are more memorable than services.
Family traditions and values are central.
“We plan together, travel together, pray together.”
“The memories with family are priceless.”
“It’s not about the app, it’s about being together.”
ImplicationDesign for families and shared experiences. Capture and celebrate shared memories.
Theme 5Waiting
Long queues created opportunities for storytelling and guided learning.
Long waiting times at temples are common.
People use waiting time to talk, learn and share stories.
Potential for guided content during wait.
“Waiting in line takes time.”
“We often share stories or listen to others.”
“If there were stories or facts, waiting would be meaningful.”
ImplicationUse waiting time meaningfully. Offer storytelling, learning and guided content in queues.

06/Concept ideation

Structure came first. Users remember emotions more than information.

Architecture is often unexplained, rituals can be confusing, and the pilgrimage ends after returning home. These became “how might we” questions.

Preserve emotional memories after pilgrimage?Explain sacred architecture without interrupting worship?Provide contextual ritual guidance?Bring pilgrimage information into one ecosystem?Support reflection and continuity?Personalise the journey while preserving agency?
01

AI spiritual companion

A guide that recommends and explains, and leaves the choice with the devotee.

Personalised travel guidance before, during and after the pilgrimage. Verified religious and heritage information is kept apart from generated suggestions, and uncertainty stays visible.

02

Heritage explorer

Learn the history and meaning of sacred places, in context.

Explains architecture, rituals and symbolism as you move through a place, with temple storytelling for the long waits in queues.

  • Reflection journal
  • Pilgrimage planner
  • Temple storytelling
  • Memory timeline
  • Calm, reverent interaction
