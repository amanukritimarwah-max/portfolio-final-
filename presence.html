4.3 · SurveyMeasuring perceived safety

A 32-item Likert-scale survey measured pedestrian safety perception across six dimensions. Cronbach’s Alpha tested whether the items in each dimension hold together: the closer to 1, the more consistent. Hover a bar for the scale.

08 · Trade-offsWhat each choice costs

Every choice costs something

Six decisions that shaped Presence. Presence’s choice is marked. Tap the other side to see what it would have meant.

Behavioural designFour principles behind the choices

The user is not “just navigating maps” and not “randomly walking alone”. Presence solves for navigating safer routes with awareness, feeling connected rather than isolated, and becoming a confident urban walker. Four behavioural principles shaped the app.

Safety feedback

Real-time alerts and cues. No panic-based design: only calm, actionable guidance.

Ownership

Users choose routes, guardians and actions. It builds confidence over dependency.

Connected support

Quick access to help when needed, as a social safety layer without intrusion.

Scarcity

Alerts only when needed. No constant notifications, which reduces anxiety and overload.

Presence transforms walking from a vulnerable activity into a supported, aware, and confidence-building experience through behavioural design and connected safety.
